Orbit
=====


.. class:: Orbit

   Describes an orbit. For example, the orbit of a vessel, obtained by calling
   :meth:`SpaceCenter.Vessel.getOrbit`, or a celestial body, obtained by calling
   :meth:`SpaceCenter.CelestialBody.getOrbit`.

   .. function:: createFromPositionAndVelocity(body, position, velocity, ut[, referenceFrame])

      Create the orbit that passes through a given position at a given velocity.
      The orbit coasts freely under the gravity of the body being orbited, so it
      describes where an object left to fall from that position and velocity would
      be at any later time.

      :param body: The celestial body being orbited.
      :type body: :class:`SpaceCenter.CelestialBody`
      :param position: The position, as a position vector.
      :type position: ``[number, number, number]``
      :param velocity: The velocity, as a vector pointing in the direction of travel, whose magnitude is the speed in :math:`\mathrm{m/s}`.
      :type velocity: ``[number, number, number]``
      :param ut: The universal time, in seconds, at which the object is at *position* traveling at *velocity*.
      :type ut: ``number``
      :param referenceFrame: The reference frame that *position* and *velocity* are in. Defaults to :meth:`SpaceCenter.CelestialBody.getNonRotatingReferenceFrame` of *body*.
      :type referenceFrame: :class:`SpaceCenter.ReferenceFrame` or ``null``
      :rtype: :class:`SpaceCenter.Orbit`
   

      .. note::

         The orbit is a single conic around *body*. It never changes
         sphere of influence, so :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.getNextOrbit` is ``null``, and no
         atmosphere slows it. A vessel held on rails follows its own orbit exactly,
         while one inside the physics bubble is simulated and drifts from it a little.

         :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.getRadius`, :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.getSpeed`, :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.position`,
         :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.velocity` and the like describe the orbit at
         *ut* and stay there. Use :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.radiusAt`,
         :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.speedAt`, :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.positionAt`, :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.velocityAt` and
         :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.trueAnomalyAtUT` for another time, and
         :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.getReferenceFrame` and :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.getOrbitalReferenceFrame` to follow
         the orbit as time passes.

         The orbit is held until :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.remove` is called on it or the client
         that created it disconnects. Remove each one created by a script that creates
         them repeatedly, for example once per update.

   .. function:: createFromOrbitalElements(body, semiMajorAxis, eccentricity, inclination, longitudeOfAscendingNode, argumentOfPeriapsis, meanAnomalyAtEpoch, epoch)

      Create the orbit with the given
      `orbital elements <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_elements>`_
      around a given body. The orbit coasts freely under the gravity of that body,
      so it describes where an object on it would be at any time.

      :param body: The celestial body being orbited.
      :type body: :class:`SpaceCenter.CelestialBody`
      :param semiMajorAxis: The semi-major axis of the orbit, in meters. Positive for an ellipse and negative for a hyperbola.
      :type semiMajorAxis: ``number``
      :param eccentricity: The eccentricity of the orbit. Below one for an ellipse and above one for a hyperbola.
      :type eccentricity: ``number``
      :param inclination: The inclination of the orbit, in radians.
      :type inclination: ``number``
      :param longitudeOfAscendingNode: The longitude of the ascending node, in radians.
      :type longitudeOfAscendingNode: ``number``
      :param argumentOfPeriapsis: The argument of periapsis, in radians.
      :type argumentOfPeriapsis: ``number``
      :param meanAnomalyAtEpoch: The mean anomaly at *epoch*, in radians.
      :type meanAnomalyAtEpoch: ``number``
      :param epoch: The universal time, in seconds, that *meanAnomalyAtEpoch* is measured at.
      :type epoch: ``number``
      :rtype: :class:`SpaceCenter.Orbit`
   

      .. note::

         The orbit is a single conic around *body*. It never changes
         sphere of influence, so :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.getNextOrbit` is ``null``, and no
         atmosphere slows it.

         The angles are measured against the reference plane and direction that
         :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.getInclination`, :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.getLongitudeOfAscendingNode` and
         :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.getArgumentOfPeriapsis` report them against.
         :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.referencePlaneNormal` and :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.referencePlaneDirection`
         give these as vectors in a reference frame.

         :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.getRadius`, :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.getSpeed`, :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.position`,
         :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.velocity` and the like describe the orbit at
         *epoch* and stay there. Use :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.radiusAt`,
         :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.speedAt`, :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.positionAt`, :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.velocityAt` and
         :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.trueAnomalyAtUT` for another time, and
         :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.getReferenceFrame` and :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.getOrbitalReferenceFrame` to follow
         the orbit as time passes.

         The orbit is held until :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.remove` is called on it or the client
         that created it disconnects. Remove each one created by a script that creates
         them repeatedly, for example once per update.

   .. method:: remove()

      Remove the orbit, releasing the memory the server holds for it.

   

      .. note::

         Only an orbit created by :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.createFromPositionAndVelocity` or
         :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.createFromOrbitalElements` can be removed. Every other orbit
         belongs to something in the game, and the server holds one of each.

         Any further use of this object throws an exception, as does use of a
         reference frame defined against it. An orbit belongs to the client that
         created it, and is removed when that client disconnects.

   .. method:: getReferenceFrame()

      The reference frame that moves along the orbit, and is orientated in a fixed
      direction.

      * The origin is at the point the orbit has reached at the
        current time.
      * The axes do not rotate. They point in the same fixed
        directions as those of
        :meth:`SpaceCenter.CelestialBody.getNonRotatingReferenceFrame` of
        :meth:`SpaceCenter.Orbit.getBody`.

      :Property: Read-only, cannot be set
      :rtype: :class:`SpaceCenter.ReferenceFrame`
   

      .. note::

         For the orbit of a vessel, the origin is the point the vessel's orbit has
         reached rather than the vessel's own center of mass. The two are a few meters
         apart, as a vessel inside the physics bubble is simulated rather than being
         held on its orbit.

   .. method:: getOrbitalReferenceFrame()

      The reference frame that moves along the orbit, and is orientated with its
      prograde/normal/radial directions.

      * The origin is at the point the orbit has reached at the
        current time.
      * The axes rotate with the orbital prograde/normal/radial
        directions.
      * The x-axis points in the orbital anti-radial direction.
      * The y-axis points in the orbital prograde direction.
      * The z-axis points in the orbital normal direction.

      :Property: Read-only, cannot be set
      :rtype: :class:`SpaceCenter.ReferenceFrame`
   

      .. note::

         For the orbit of a vessel, the origin is the point the vessel's orbit has
         reached rather than the vessel's own center of mass, which is what
         :meth:`SpaceCenter.Vessel.getOrbitalReferenceFrame` is centered on.

   .. method:: getSpecificEnergy()

      The specific orbital energy, in :math:`\mathrm{J/kg}`.

      :Property: Read-only, cannot be set
      :rtype: ``number``
   

      .. note::

         This is the sum of the orbit's specific kinetic and potential energy,
         :math:`v^{2}/2 - \mu/r`, where :math:`v` is the orbital speed,
         :math:`\mu` the gravitational parameter of the body and :math:`r` the
         orbital radius. It is negative for a bound (elliptical) orbit.
