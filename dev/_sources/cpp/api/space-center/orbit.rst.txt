Orbit
=====


.. class:: Orbit

   Describes an orbit. For example, the orbit of a vessel, obtained by calling
   :func:`Vessel::orbit`, or a celestial body, obtained by calling
   :func:`CelestialBody::orbit`.

   .. function:: static Orbit create_from_position_and_velocity(Client& connection, CelestialBody body, std::tuple<double, double, double> position, std::tuple<double, double, double> velocity, double ut, ReferenceFrame reference_frame)

      Create the orbit that passes through a given position at a given velocity.
      The orbit coasts freely under the gravity of the body being orbited, so it
      describes where an object left to fall from that position and velocity would
      be at any later time.

      :Parameters:

       * **body** -- The celestial body being orbited.
       * **position** -- The position, as a position vector.
       * **velocity** -- The velocity, as a vector pointing in the direction of travel, whose magnitude is the speed in meters per second.
       * **ut** -- The universal time, in seconds, at which the object is at *position* traveling at *velocity*.
       * **reference_frame** -- The reference frame that *position* and *velocity* are in. Defaults to :func:`CelestialBody::non_rotating_reference_frame` of *body*.

   

      .. note::

         The orbit is a single conic around *body*. It never changes
         sphere of influence, so :func:`Orbit::next_orbit` is ``NULL``, and no
         atmosphere slows it. A vessel held on rails follows its own orbit exactly,
         while one inside the physics bubble is simulated and drifts from it a little.

         :func:`Orbit::radius`, :func:`Orbit::speed`, :func:`Orbit::true_anomaly`,
         :func:`Orbit::time_to_apoapsis` and the like describe the orbit at
         *ut* and stay there. Use :func:`Orbit::radius_at`,
         :func:`Orbit::speed_at`, :func:`Orbit::position_at`, :func:`Orbit::velocity_at` and
         :func:`Orbit::true_anomaly_at_ut` for another time, and
         :func:`Orbit::reference_frame` and :func:`Orbit::orbital_reference_frame` to follow
         the orbit as time passes.

         The orbit is held until :func:`Orbit::remove` is called on it or the client
         that created it disconnects. Remove each one created by a script that creates
         them repeatedly, for example once per update.

   .. function:: static Orbit create_from_orbital_elements(Client& connection, CelestialBody body, double semi_major_axis, double eccentricity, double inclination, double longitude_of_ascending_node, double argument_of_periapsis, double mean_anomaly_at_epoch, double epoch)

      Create the orbit with the given
      `orbital elements <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_elements>`_
      around a given body. The orbit coasts freely under the gravity of that body,
      so it describes where an object on it would be at any time.

      :Parameters:

       * **body** -- The celestial body being orbited.
       * **semi_major_axis** -- The semi-major axis of the orbit, in meters. Positive for an ellipse and negative for a hyperbola.
       * **eccentricity** -- The eccentricity of the orbit. Below one for an ellipse and above one for a hyperbola.
       * **inclination** -- The inclination of the orbit, in radians.
       * **longitude_of_ascending_node** -- The longitude of the ascending node, in radians.
       * **argument_of_periapsis** -- The argument of periapsis, in radians.
       * **mean_anomaly_at_epoch** -- The mean anomaly at *epoch*, in radians.
       * **epoch** -- The universal time, in seconds, that *mean_anomaly_at_epoch* is measured at.

   

      .. note::

         The orbit is a single conic around *body*. It never changes
         sphere of influence, so :func:`Orbit::next_orbit` is ``NULL``, and no
         atmosphere slows it.

         The angles are measured against the reference plane and direction that
         :func:`Orbit::inclination`, :func:`Orbit::longitude_of_ascending_node` and
         :func:`Orbit::argument_of_periapsis` report them against.
         :func:`Orbit::reference_plane_normal` and :func:`Orbit::reference_plane_direction`
         give these as vectors in a reference frame.

         :func:`Orbit::radius`, :func:`Orbit::speed`, :func:`Orbit::true_anomaly`,
         :func:`Orbit::time_to_apoapsis` and the like describe the orbit at
         *epoch* and stay there. Use :func:`Orbit::radius_at`,
         :func:`Orbit::speed_at`, :func:`Orbit::position_at`, :func:`Orbit::velocity_at` and
         :func:`Orbit::true_anomaly_at_ut` for another time, and
         :func:`Orbit::reference_frame` and :func:`Orbit::orbital_reference_frame` to follow
         the orbit as time passes.

         The orbit is held until :func:`Orbit::remove` is called on it or the client
         that created it disconnects. Remove each one created by a script that creates
         them repeatedly, for example once per update.

   .. function:: void remove()

      Remove the orbit, releasing the memory the server holds for it.


   

      .. note::

         Only an orbit created by :func:`Orbit::create_from_position_and_velocity` or
         :func:`Orbit::create_from_orbital_elements` can be removed. Every other orbit
         belongs to something in the game, and the server holds one of each.

         Any further use of this object throws an exception, as does use of a
         reference frame defined against it. An orbit belongs to the client that
         created it, and is removed when that client disconnects.

   .. function:: CelestialBody body()

      The celestial body (e.g. planet or moon) around which the object is orbiting.

   

   .. function:: ReferenceFrame reference_frame()

      The reference frame that moves along the orbit, and is orientated in a fixed
      direction.

      * The origin is at the point the orbit has reached at the
        current time.
      * The axes do not rotate. They point in the same fixed
        directions as those of
        :func:`CelestialBody::non_rotating_reference_frame` of
        :func:`Orbit::body`.

   

      .. note::

         For the orbit of a vessel, the origin is the point the vessel's orbit has
         reached rather than the vessel's own center of mass. The two are a few meters
         apart, as a vessel inside the physics bubble is simulated rather than being
         held on its orbit.

   .. function:: ReferenceFrame orbital_reference_frame()

      The reference frame that moves along the orbit, and is orientated with its
      prograde/normal/radial directions.

      * The origin is at the point the orbit has reached at the
        current time.
      * The axes rotate with the orbital prograde/normal/radial
        directions.
      * The x-axis points in the orbital anti-radial direction.
      * The y-axis points in the orbital prograde direction.
      * The z-axis points in the orbital normal direction.

   

      .. note::

         For the orbit of a vessel, the origin is the point the vessel's orbit has
         reached rather than the vessel's own center of mass, which is what
         :func:`Vessel::orbital_reference_frame` is centered on.

   .. function:: double apoapsis()

      Gets the apoapsis of the orbit, in meters, from the center of mass
      of the body being orbited.

   

      .. note::

         For the apoapsis altitude reported on the in-game map view,
         use :func:`Orbit::apoapsis_altitude`.

   .. function:: double periapsis()

      The periapsis of the orbit, in meters, from the center of mass
      of the body being orbited.

   

      .. note::

         For the periapsis altitude reported on the in-game map view,
         use :func:`Orbit::periapsis_altitude`.

   .. function:: double apoapsis_altitude()

      The apoapsis of the orbit, in meters, above the sea level of the body being orbited.

   

      .. note::

         This is equal to :func:`Orbit::apoapsis` minus the equatorial radius of the body.

   .. function:: double periapsis_altitude()

      The periapsis of the orbit, in meters, above the sea level of the body being orbited.

   

      .. note::

         This is equal to :func:`Orbit::periapsis` minus the equatorial radius of the body.

      The semi-minor axis of the orbit, in meters.

   

   .. function:: double radius()

      The current radius of the orbit, in meters. This is the distance between the center
      of mass of the object in orbit, and the center of mass of the body around which it
      is orbiting.

   

      .. note::

         This value will change over time if the orbit is elliptical.

   .. function:: double specific_energy()

      The specific orbital energy, in Joules per kilogram (equivalently,
      meters squared per second squared).

   

      .. note::

         This is the sum of the orbit's specific kinetic and potential energy, and
         is negative for a bound (elliptical) orbit.

      .. note::

   .. function:: double argument_of_periapsis()
